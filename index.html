Clause 1 — Contract Review

Spellbook

AI redlining, inside Microsoft Word

Spellbook drops an AI reviewer directly into the Word document you already use to send and receive contracts, instead of pulling you into a separate app. It reads the clause you're looking at, flags what's missing or unusual against your own playbook, and drafts a redline you can accept, edit, or reject line by line — the same motion as reviewing a colleague's markup.

For a solo lawyer or a two-person firm, the pitch is narrow and specific: it doesn't try to manage your whole contract lifecycle, it makes the highest-billable-hour task — first-pass review — faster. That narrowness is why it's worth testing before a heavier CLM platform.

Best for: solo practitioners and small firms doing high volumes of NDAs, MSAs, and vendor agreements who live in Word already.

Clause 2 — Proposals, Contracts & Signing

PandaDoc

Send it, sign it, get paid — one thread

Where Spellbook is a scalpel for the review step, PandaDoc is the whole pipeline after the contract is agreed: templated proposals, e-signature, and — for firms that bill on delivery — payment collection tied to the signed document. It's less "legal AI" and more "the paperwork stops living in three different inboxes."

For a consultant or small firm sending the same three document types on repeat — engagement letters, retainers, statements of work — the templating is where the time actually gets saved, not the AI layer.

Best for: consultants and small firms who want one place to send, sign, and get paid on standard documents.

Questions we keep getting

Before you switch anything.

Does a solo lawyer actually need AI contract software?

Only if first-pass review is a real chunk of your billable week. If you review fewer than a handful of contracts a month, the time saved won't outpace the subscription cost — a template library might do the job instead.

Is PandaDoc good enough to replace a lawyer's engagement-letter process?

For standard engagement letters and retainers, yes — the templating and e-signature cover it. For anything requiring clause-by-clause negotiation, pair it with a review tool like Spellbook rather than expecting PandaDoc to redline for you.

How much does Navan cost for a two-person firm?

The core expense and booking platform has a free tier; you mainly pay through the travel booked, not a flat software fee. It only makes sense if travel is a recurring cost, not an occasional one.

Can I use these tools together, or should I pick one?

They cover different steps — Spellbook for review, PandaDoc for sending and signing, Navan for travel — so there's no overlap that would make running more than one wasteful. Start with whichever step costs you the most hours today.
