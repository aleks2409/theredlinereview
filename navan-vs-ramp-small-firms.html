Navan vs Ramp for Small Firms: Which One Should Handle Your Travel and Expenses?

Both tools promise free travel booking and automatic expense reports. They come at the problem from opposite ends: Navan is a travel platform that added expenses, Ramp is a corporate card that added travel. For a 1–20 person law, accounting or consulting firm, that difference decides which one fits.

UPDATED NOV 2026 8 MIN READ CATEGORY: TRAVEL & EXPENSE
The short answer Pick Navan if travel is the main reason you want a tool, if you want to keep the cards you already have, or if your firm is a sole proprietorship. Pick Ramp if you want to replace your company cards and bill-pay process with one free platform, your firm is a registered corporation, LLC or LP, and you keep at least $25,000 in a US business bank account. Travel booking carries no booking fee on either platform.

Navan: a travel platform that also does expenses

Navan started as a corporate travel booking tool, and travel is still the center of the product. Flights, hotels and cars are booked in the same app where expenses are reported, so a booked trip shows up on the expense side without anyone forwarding a confirmation email.

The pricing is the main reason it suits a small firm. According to Navan's pricing page, travel is free for companies with 300 or fewer employees, with no platform fee and no cap on the number of trips. Expense management is free for the first five monthly expensing users and $15 per user per month after that. Navan counts an active expense user as anyone who submits a transaction in a given month, so a ten-person firm where only four people travel would still sit inside the free allowance. The pricing page also lists 24/7 travel support agents on the free tier, which matters when a flight to a deposition is cancelled at 6 a.m.

The other practical point is cards. With Navan Connect, a firm can link the Visa, Mastercard or American Express corporate cards it already uses, and transactions flow into Navan Expense. You do not have to switch banks or card programs to get automatic expense capture. Navan's own release notes suggest confirming that your specific card program is eligible.

Ramp: a corporate card that also books travel

Ramp is a spend management platform built around its own corporate cards. Its free plan covers unlimited physical and virtual cards with spend limits, expense policy enforcement, bill pay, and integrations with QuickBooks Online and Xero, according to Ramp's small-business guide. Ramp Plus starts at $15 per user per month plus a platform fee based on team size, and mainly adds advanced accounts-payable and accounting automation features. Ramp does not publish the platform fee amount.

Ramp Travel launched in June 2024 through a partnership with Priceline. Ramp says it charges no platform or booking fees regardless of the number of employees or trips, and that travel is included on all plans. Admins can set rules such as price caps, advance-booking windows, cabin class and refundable-only hotels. When an employee picks an option outside policy, the booking turns into an approval request instead of going through. Ramp's help center says multi-city trips are not supported (it suggests booking separate one-way flights) and that group trips cannot be booked in one go yet, which matters if a partner regularly visits several clients in one trip.

The catch for small firms is eligibility. Ramp's US qualification page requires a business registered in the United States as a corporation, LLC or LP, at least $25,000 in cash in a linked US business bank account, and a physical US address (not a PO box, virtual office or registered agent address). It does not accept sole proprietors. A solo practitioner operating as a sole proprietor cannot use Ramp at all, and a small firm with a lean operating account may not meet the cash requirement. If your firm is a PLLC or LLP, confirm with Ramp whether your entity type qualifies before you plan around it.

Where the difference actually shows up

If travel is the problem

Navan is the more complete travel product: travel is the core of the platform, it publishes a 24/7 support line on the free tier, and Ramp documents gaps around multi-city and group trips. For a firm whose people fly to clients, court or audits every month, that is the deciding factor.

If cards and bills are the problem

Ramp covers more of the back office for free. If you want to replace a mix of personal cards, a single shared company card and manual vendor payments, Ramp's free plan bundles cards, expenses and bill pay.

If you want to keep your bank

Navan Connect works with existing Visa, Mastercard and Amex corporate cards. Ramp's product is built around its own cards, so getting the most out of it generally means adopting Ramp's card program.

Which one fits your firm

Quick decision guide
  • Solo practitioner or sole proprietorship: Navan. Ramp does not accept sole proprietors.
  • 2–20 people, regular client or court travel, happy with current cards: Navan, with your existing cards linked through Navan Connect.

  • Frequent multi-city trips: Navan, until Ramp supports multi-city booking.
  • Fewer than one trip a month and no card problems: neither yet. A spreadsheet is still fine.

Frequently asked questions

Is Navan or Ramp cheaper for a small firm?

For most firms of 1–20 people, both can cost nothing. Navan's travel booking is free up to 300 employees and expenses are free for five monthly active users; Ramp's free plan includes cards, expenses, bill pay and travel. Navan starts charging when more than five people submit expenses in a month ($15 each); Ramp charges when you need Ramp Plus features ($15 per user per month plus a platform fee).

Can a solo lawyer or accountant use Ramp?

Not as a sole proprietor. Ramp's US qualification page says it does not accept individuals, sole proprietors or other unregistered businesses. A solo practice registered as a corporation or LLC may qualify if it also meets the $25,000 cash requirement.

Do I need Ramp's card to use Ramp?

Ramp's platform is built around its own corporate cards, so plan on adopting them. If you want to keep your current bank's cards, Navan Connect is designed for exactly that.

Can either tool track travel by client or matter?

Both let you categorize spend so it can be mapped to a client or matter. For law firms, see our guide on how small law firms track client travel expenses, and check field options during a trial.
