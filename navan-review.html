Navan Review: Is It Worth It for a Solo Firm or Small Practice?

Navan bundles travel booking and expense tracking into one tool. For a firm that travels a few times a year, that might be overkill. For one that travels monthly, it can quietly save hours you didn't know you were losing.

UPDATED AUG 2026 8 MIN READ CATEGORY: TRAVEL & EXPENSE
The short answer Navan is worth setting up if travel is a recurring line item on your books — client visits, court appearances in another city, conferences. If you travel once or twice a year, a normal expense spreadsheet is still faster to maintain than learning a new tool.

What Navan actually does

Navan combines two things that are usually separate: a travel booking platform (flights, hotels, rental cars) and an expense management system. The pitch is that when someone on your team books a flight through Navan, the receipt and cost already land in the expense report — nobody has to photograph a receipt or manually categorize a line item afterward.

For a solo practitioner, that mostly matters at tax time and when reconciling a card statement. For a small firm with two or three people who travel, it matters every week, because someone is otherwise stuck chasing receipts from whoever just got back from a deposition three states away.

Who this is actually for

Based on what the tool is built to do, it fits best for:

  • Small firms with recurring travel — regular court appearances outside your home city, recurring client site visits, or conference circuits.
  • Firms already tracking expenses manually and finding it eats into billable time, especially around month-end reconciliation.
  • Anyone who currently reimburses travel out of pocket and wants a company card + booking flow instead of chasing personal receipts.

It's a weaker fit if your practice rarely sends anyone out of town — the setup time isn't worth it for two trips a year.

A realistic scenario

Example

Say a two-partner firm sends someone out of town for a deposition or client meeting roughly twice a month. Under a manual process, that's two sets of flight and hotel receipts, a card statement to reconcile, and an expense summary someone has to build by hand before it goes to the bookkeeper — often 30–45 minutes of unbilled admin work per trip.

With booking and expense tracking in one flow, that admin step mostly disappears — the trip is already categorized when it's booked. Over a year of two trips a month, that's roughly 12–18 hours of admin time that goes back into billable work instead.

That's an illustrative estimate, not a guarantee — the actual time saved depends on how disorganized your current process is. A firm that already has a tight expense workflow will see less benefit than one still using a shared spreadsheet.

Where it falls short

How it compares to just using a spreadsheet

A spreadsheet costs nothing and works fine at low volume. The trade-off shows up as trip frequency increases: each additional trip adds the same fixed chunk of manual work — collecting receipts, categorizing them, matching them to the right client or matter. Navan's booking-to-expense flow removes that repeated step, so the time saved compounds with volume rather than staying flat. The break-even point is roughly around one out-of-town trip a month; below that, the spreadsheet is still faster to maintain than learning a new tool.

Frequently asked questions

Is Navan only for large companies?

No — the core platform has a free base plan and same-day setup, which makes it accessible to a two- or three-person firm, not just enterprise travel teams.

Does Navan replace my accountant or bookkeeper?

No. It organizes and categorizes travel expenses so your bookkeeper has clean data to work with — it doesn't replace the bookkeeping itself.

What if my firm only travels a few times a year?

At that volume, the time saved likely won't outweigh the effort of switching tools. A spreadsheet or your existing expense process is probably still the faster option.

Is this a legal-industry-specific tool?

No — Navan is a general business travel and expense tool. It earns a place on this site because the underlying problem (manual paperwork eating billable time) is the same one our contract-review recommendations solve, just for travel instead of documents.
